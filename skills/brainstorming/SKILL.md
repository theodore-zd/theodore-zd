# Brainstorming

Help the user work out what they want and why. Keep the evolving brainstorm in the conversation. When the thinking is confirmed, update the wiki pages it affects.

## Get oriented

Read the material the user points to and the relevant project docs or code before asking substantive questions. Identify what already exists, what the user has settled, and which uncertainty would most change the outcome. Handle clear, routine requests directly unless the user asks to brainstorm.

Open by reflecting the goal and any consequential finding from the material you read. If the purpose is unclear, ask an open question in the user's terms and explain what the answer would change. Let them describe the outcome before suggesting approaches.

## Develop the idea

- Ask one consequential question at a time. Batch a few independent questions when that saves a round trip. Explore intent, audience, success, constraints, and outcomes to avoid as they become relevant.
- Play back what changed as the conversation progresses. Distinguish confirmed decisions, your proposals, and open questions. Park decisions the user wants to defer.
- Once the purpose is clear, compare the viable approaches with concrete tradeoffs grounded in the project. Recommend one and explain why. When the user delegates a choice, make it and label it as your recommendation.
- Use a quick text sketch or diagram when it makes a layout, flow, or relationship easier to discuss. Keep examples at the level needed to make the decision.

## Confirm the final thinking

When the goal and direction are clear and any consequential open questions have been resolved or explicitly deferred, play back the outcome in chat. Summarize the intent, agreed direction and rationale, constraints, and remaining open questions. Ask the user to confirm or correct it. Incorporate corrections and get confirmation of the revised outcome before editing the wiki. An incomplete brainstorm can stay in the conversation until the user resumes it.

## Update the wiki

After confirmation, find the existing wiki pages affected by the outcome. Start with the system model when the decision changes a system's responsibilities, boundaries, behavior, or relationships. Read the wiki's system definitions and follow its terminology, page structure, and conventions. Use the wiki's existing references to locate the right pages.

Edit the smallest relevant set of pages. Record the confirmed decisions and their rationale where they belong. Keep deferred questions visibly open if they matter to future readers. Preserve unrelated content and distinguish agreed future behavior from the current system. Leave the wiki as it is if the brainstorm produced no durable change. If the relevant wiki is unavailable, report that and preserve the confirmed summary in chat.

Finish with a short recap and links to the pages updated. The brainstorm ends there.
